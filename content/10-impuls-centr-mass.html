1. The problem

The second law $\sum\vec F = m\vec a$ holds for a point mass in an inertial reference frame. We do not yet have equations for the motion of an extended body.

Solution: divide the system into $N \gg 1$ small parts and write the second law for each.

2. Internal and external forces

  • Internal forces: interactions between bodies inside the system.
  • External forces: interactions of bodies of the system with bodies outside it.
§mom_system§ $$\begin{aligned} m_1\vec a_1 &= \sum_{i\ne1}\vec F_{1i} + \vec F_1^{\,\text{ext}}\\ m_2\vec a_2 &= \sum_{i\ne2}\vec F_{2i} + \vec F_2^{\,\text{ext}}\\ &\;\;\vdots\\ m_N\vec a_N &= \sum_{i\ne N}\vec F_{Ni} + \vec F_N^{\,\text{ext}} \end{aligned}$$

Add all the equations. By the third law $\vec F_{ij} = -\vec F_{ji}$, so all internal forces cancel in pairs:

Result$$\sum_{i=1}^N m_i\vec a_i = \sum_{i=1}^N \vec F_i^{\,\text{ext}}$$

3. Centre of mass (gravity)

Where should a light rod with loads be supported so that it does not turn? The moment of the total weight applied at $x_C$ must equal the sum of the moments of the separate loads:

$$m_1gx_1 + m_2gx_2 + \ldots + m_Ngx_N = (m_1+\ldots+m_N)\,g\,x_C.$$ §mom_center§ $$x_C = \frac{\sum m_ix_i}{\sum m_i} = \frac{\sum m_i x_i}{M}.$$

For two equal masses the centre is midway between them. For several bodies one can first replace a pair by a single point with the total mass and then add the next one.

Three equal masses at the vertices of a triangle: replace two by a single mass $2m$ at the middle of a side; the centre of mass divides the segment from that point to the third mass in the ratio $1:2$.

4. Conservation of momentum

Important addition

The notes announce the topic “Conservation of momentum”, but the derivation stops at the sum $m_i\vec a_i$. Here is the rest:

The momentum of the system is $\vec p = \sum m_i\vec v_i$. Since $\vec a_i = d\vec v_i/dt$,

$$\frac{d\vec p}{dt} = \sum\vec F^{\,\text{ext}}.$$

If there are no external forces or their sum is zero, then $\vec p = \text{const}$: conservation of momentum. It explains the experiment with the carts: starting from rest, $m_1v_1 = m_2v_2$.

Differentiating the definition of the centre of mass twice, we get

$$M\vec a_C = \sum\vec F^{\,\text{ext}}:$$

the centre of mass moves like a point of mass $M$ with all the external forces applied to it.
