1. Vector

A vector is a directed line segment; strictly, the class of all equal directed segments (an equivalence class). A vector has two characteristics: magnitude (length) and direction. The magnitude of $\overrightarrow{AB}$ is written $|\overrightarrow{AB}| = AB$.

Two kinds of quantities

Vector quantities have a direction: velocity $\vec v$, force $\vec F$, acceleration $\vec a$.
Scalar quantities have no direction: distance, time, mass.

2. Operations on vectors

Multiplication by a number

For $\lambda \in \mathbb R$ the vector $\lambda\vec a$ has magnitude $|\lambda|\cdot|\vec a|$. If $\lambda \gt 0$ it points the same way as $\vec a$ ($\uparrow\uparrow$), if $\lambda \lt 0$ the opposite way ($\uparrow\downarrow$).

Sum and difference

Triangle rule: put the tail of $\vec b$ at the head of $\vec a$; the sum $\vec c = \vec a + \vec b$ goes from the tail of $\vec a$ to the head of $\vec b$. Parallelogram rule: draw both vectors from one point, the sum is the diagonal.

A difference reduces to a sum: $\vec a - \vec b = \vec a + (-1)\cdot\vec b$.

3. Coordinates and the position vector

Before, a point $A$ was given by a pair of numbers $(x_A, y_A)$. Now we write the same thing as a single vector from the origin, the position vector: $\vec r_A = \overrightarrow{OA}$.

If $\vec i$ and $\vec j$ are the unit vectors of the axes, $|\vec i| = |\vec j| = 1$, then

$$\vec r_A = x_A\,\vec i + y_A\,\vec j = \begin{pmatrix} x_A \\ y_A \end{pmatrix}.$$

4. Projection onto an axis

The projection $a_x$ of a vector $\vec a$ onto the $Ox$ axis is a number:

$$a_x = x_\text{end} - x_\text{start}.$$
  • $a_x \gt 0$ if the vector points along the axis;
  • $a_x \lt 0$ if it points against the axis;
  • $a_x = 0$ if $\vec a \perp Ox$.

5. Problems

Does the sum depend on the order of the terms?

No. On grid paper every vector is “so many squares right and so many up”. Adding vectors just adds these shifts, and a sum of numbers does not depend on order.

Example from the notebook: $\vec a$ and $\vec b$ in either order give the same vector, 6 squares right and 8 up. $\vec a - \vec b$ and $\vec a + \vec b + \vec c$ are built square by square in the same way.

Construct 5a + 3b and −a − 2b

Draw the vectors as a chain: each one starts where the previous one ends. The result goes from the start of the first to the end of the last. A minus sign reverses a vector.

Signs of projections

A trick for any vector in a drawing: look separately at where it shifts horizontally (the sign of $a_x$) and vertically (the sign of $a_y$). A vector parallel to one axis has zero projection onto the other.
