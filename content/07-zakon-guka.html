1. Deformations and the normal force

Kinds of deformation: tension / compression, bending, torsion, shear. A body tends to keep its shape, and this is where elastic forces come from.

§hooke_types§

The normal force $\vec N$ is the elastic force of a support, perpendicular to the surface (both on a horizontal table and on a curved surface).

2. Hooke's law

$$F \sim \Delta l, \qquad F = k\,\Delta l, \qquad k = \frac{F}{\Delta l}\ \ [\text{N/m}].$$

$k$ is the spring constant (stiffness).

  • Elastic deformation: after the force is removed the body returns to its original shape.
  • Plastic (inelastic): after the force is removed the shape is not restored.
Correction

In the notebook the definitions are labelled the other way round: “returns to its original shape” describes elastic deformation.

Up to the limit of proportionality the graph is a straight line and Hooke's law holds. Then, in order: the elastic limit, yielding, ultimate strength, fracture.

3. Spring combinations

§hooke_connect§

In series the springs share the force and the extensions add: $\Delta l = \dfrac{F}{k_1}+\dfrac{F}{k_2}$. In parallel the extension is common and the forces add.

4. A spring stretched by its own weight

The tension differs from place to place in a hanging spring. Cut it mentally into $N \gg 1$ equal pieces of mass $m_1 = m/N$. The pieces are connected in series, so

$$\frac1k = \frac{N}{k_1} \;\Rightarrow\; k_1 = Nk.$$ §hooke_pieces§

The bottom piece carries no load, the next one holds one piece, then two, three … For the $i$-th piece from the bottom:

$$\Delta l_i = \frac{(i-1)\,m_1 g}{k_1}.$$

Add them up (neglecting the stretching of a piece under its own weight):

$$\Delta l = \frac{m_1g}{k_1}\big(0+1+\ldots+(N-1)\big) = \frac{m_1 g}{2k_1}N(N-1) = \frac{mg}{2k}\cdot\frac{N-1}{N} \xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{} \frac{mg}{2k}.$$
Result$$L = l_0 + \frac{mg}{2k}$$

A heavy spring stretches as if all its weight were concentrated at its middle.

5. Experiment: the spring constant

The spring has a mass of $31.4$ g and $47$ turns, one turn $\approx 0.67$ g. Measuring only the full length is inaccurate, but one can hang different numbers of turns $n$. Let $l_1$ be the unloaded length of a turn, $m_1, k_1$ the mass and stiffness of one turn:

$$L_n = n\,l_1 + \frac{m_1 g}{2k_1}\,n^2.$$

6. Young's modulus

For a rod of length $l$ and cross-section $A$: two equal rods end to end are softer ($k \propto 1/l$), side by side they are stiffer ($k\propto A$).

§hooke_rod§ §hooke_young§

Combining the two dependences gives

$$k = E\,\frac{A}{l},$$

where $E$ is Young's modulus, which depends only on the material.

7. Problems

§hooke_tasks§

1. Mixed combination

A 10 N/m spring, then two parallel ones of 10 N/m each (20 together), then another 10 N/m:

$$\frac1k = \frac1{10}+\frac1{20}+\frac1{10} = \frac{5}{20} \;\Rightarrow\; k = 4\ \text{N/m}.$$

2. A spring and a pulley

A pulley hangs on a spring; a string with one fixed end goes around it and we pull the free end. The spring carries $2F$ and stretches by $2F/k$, and the string end moves twice as far, $4F/k$. The equivalent stiffness is $k_\text{eq} = k/4$.

3. Two springs and a pulley

If the lower end of the string is also fixed through a spring $k$: the upper one stretches by $\Delta x_1 = 2F/k$, the lower one by $\Delta x_2 = F/k$, and the string end moves $2\Delta x_1+\Delta x_2 = 5F/k$. So $k_\text{eq} = k/5$.

4. A vessel on a spring

§hooke_vessel§

1000 cm³ of a liquid of density 0.8 g/cm³ is 800 g, that is 8 N. An extra 8 N deforms the spring by 10 cm, 16 N by 20 cm: $k = 8/0.1 = 80$ N/m.

A side fact about the movable pulley

The speeds are related by $2u = v_1 + v_2$, where $u$ is the speed of the pulley axle and $v_1, v_2$ are the speeds of the string ends (with signs).

§hooke_pulley_speed§

5. Stiffness from a load

A 1 N load stretches a spring by 10 cm: $k = \dfrac{F}{\Delta l} = \dfrac{1}{0.1} = 10$ N/m.
