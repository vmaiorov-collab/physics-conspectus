1. Two masses

Gravitational mass $m_\text{g}$ shows how strong the gravitational force on a body is (at one and the same place in space).

Inertia is the property of bodies to keep their motion. To change the motion, a force must be applied. Inertial mass $m_\text{in}$ shows how strong the inertia of a body is.

Gravitational mass is compared with a standard on a balance. Inertial mass is compared by how two carts fly apart when a spring between them is released:

$$\frac{m_1}{m_2} = \frac{v_2}{v_1}, \qquad m_1v_1 = m_2v_2.$$ §mass_two§
Einstein's equivalence principle$$m_\text{in} = m_\text{g} = m$$

3. Problems

1. Iron and aluminium rods of equal mass

§dens_rods§

With equal mass and cross-section $\rho_\text{Fe}\,A\,l_\text{Fe} = \rho_\text{Al}\,A\,l_\text{Al}$ (here $A$ is the cross-sectional area). The aluminium rod is longer:

$$\frac{l_\text{Al}}{l_\text{Fe}} = \frac{7800}{2700} = \frac{26}{9}\approx 2.9.$$

2. Density of jam

§dens_jar§

A 3 L jar with jam weighs 3.9 kg, the empty jar 400 g. The net mass is 3.5 kg:

$$\rho = \frac{3.5\ \text{kg}}{3\cdot10^{-3}\ \text{m}^3} \approx 1170\ \text{kg/m}^3.$$

3. How many cartloads are needed to remove 5000 m³

$m = \rho V = 1500\cdot5000 = 7.5\cdot10^6$ kg $= 7500$ t. With a capacity of 20 t: $N = 7500/20 = 375$.

4. How long it takes to unload a 2 × 1.5 × 1.8 m truck body

§dens_truck§

$V = 5.4$ m³. A rate of 20 kg/min at density 800 kg/m³ is $\tfrac1{40}$ m³/min. So $t = 5.4\cdot40 = 216$ min $= 3.6$ h.

5. Hollow brass cube

§dens_hollow§

The surface area is $216$ cm² $= 6a^2$, so the edge is $a = 6$ cm. The walls are 2 mm thick, so the cavity edge is $5.6$ cm:

$$V = 6^3 - 5.6^3 = 216 - 175.616 = 40.384\ \text{cm}^3, \qquad m = 8.5\ \text{g/cm}^3\cdot V \approx 343\ \text{g}.$$

6. A cube without a “corner”

§dens_corner§

A cube of edge $a/2$ is cut out of a cube of edge $a$:

$$m = \rho\Big(a^3 - \big(\tfrac a2\big)^3\Big) = \frac78\rho a^3.$$

7. A birch model and the real iron part

§dens_scale§

A model of mass 100 g is made to a 1:5 scale. The volumes are in the ratio $1:5^3 = 1:125$.

$$M = m\cdot125\cdot\frac{\rho_\text{Fe}}{\rho_\text{birch}} = 0.1\cdot125\cdot\frac{7800}{650} = 150\ \text{kg}.$$

Another way. The model volume is $V = 0.1/650 = 1/6500$ m³, the part is $125$ times bigger: $1/52$ m³. Its mass is $m = 7800/52 = 150$ kg.

8. A cube with holes

§dens_holes§

The holes remove 7 small cubes out of 27. If a cube of edge $a$ has mass $m$, then a cube of edge $3a$ without these seven has $M = (27-7)m = 20m$.

9. Heating a body

§dens_heat§

All sizes grew by 2%: the length scale is $1.02$, the volume scale $1.02^3 \approx 1.06$. The mass is unchanged, so the density decreased by a factor of 1.06:

$$\frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1} = \frac{1}{1.06}\approx 0.94.$$

10. Can twenty people carry this ingot?

§dens_gold§

A gold ingot of about 1216 kg has volume $V = M/\rho = 1216/19300 \approx 0.063$ m³. That is a cube with edge $\sqrt[3]{0.063}\approx 0.40$ m. No: only a few people can get hold of such a cube, and each would have to take about 300 kg.

11. Average density of a coin

§dens_coin§

$m = 8.5$ g, diameter $D = 25.75$ mm, thickness $h = 2.2$ mm:

$$\rho = \frac{4m}{\pi D^2 h} \approx 7.4\cdot10^3\ \text{kg/m}^3.$$

This is less than the density of any metal in the coin. The reason: $h$ is the largest thickness (at the rim), while the middle with the design is thinner, so the real volume is smaller than the calculated one.
