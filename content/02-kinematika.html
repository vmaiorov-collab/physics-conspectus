1. What kinematics studies

Kinematics describes the motion of bodies without discussing its causes (no forces at all).

  • Trajectory: the line along which a body moves over time.
  • Distance $S$: the length of the trajectory, a scalar, $[S] = \text{m}$.
  • Displacement $\vec d$: the vector of the change of position, from the starting point to the final one.
§kin_path§
Inequality

$S \ge |\vec d|$: the shortest path between two points is a straight segment. Equality holds only for motion along a straight line in one direction.

2. Speed and velocity

Average speed says how fast you moved, while average velocity also says in which direction:

$$\langle v\rangle = \frac{S}{t}, \qquad \langle\vec v\rangle = \frac{\vec d}{t}.$$

If the speed changes, take a very short interval; this gives the instantaneous speed and velocity:

$$v = \lim_{\Delta t\to0}\frac{\Delta S}{\Delta t}, \qquad \vec v = \lim_{\Delta t\to0}\frac{\Delta\vec d}{\Delta t}.$$

3. Distance–time graph S(t)

  • $S(t) \ge 0$ and $\dfrac{dS}{dt} \ge 0$ at every moment: distance never decreases.
  • Speed is the gradient of the tangent, $v = \dfrac{dS}{dt}$.
  • Average speed is the gradient of the secant, $\langle v\rangle = S_\text{total}/t_\text{total}$.

5. Displacement graph $d_x(t)$

  • $d_x$ can be negative;
  • the derivative $\dfrac{d}{dt}d_x(t)$ can be negative too: the body moves against the axis;
  • $\langle v_x\rangle = \dfrac{d_{x}}{t}$ over the whole time;
  • $v_x$ is the derivative (slope) of $d_x(t)$.
§kin_dx§

From position to distance and speed

One graph $x(t)$ gives three others:

  • distance $d(t)$: where the position decreases, that part of the graph is “reflected” upwards, since distance only grows;
  • velocity component $v_x(t)$ is the slope of $x(t)$; it is zero at the turning points;
  • speed $v(t) = |v_x(t)|$.

6. Velocity graph $v_x(t)$

Key property

The area under the $v_x(t)$ graph equals the displacement. The part below the axis counts with a minus sign.

§kin_area§

In uniform motion the speed depends neither on time nor on position: the graph $v(x)$ is a horizontal segment, for example $v = 4$ m/s for $x$ from $-4$ to $4$ m.

7. Problem: hill versus dip

Two balls start with the same speed $s$. The first track has a hill (the ball slows down on it), the second has a dip of the same shape (the ball speeds up in it). Which one is faster?

§kin_hill§

Let $A \gt 0$ be the area by which the graph deviates from the constant speed. In the same time $t$ the balls travel

$$\int_0^t T(t)\,dt = st - A, \qquad \int_0^t B(t)\,dt = st + A.$$

By contradiction: if equal distances were covered in equal time, then $st - A = st + A$, so $A = 0$. But then there would be neither a hill nor a dip, a contradiction. Since $-A \lt A$, the ball on the track with the dip gets further in the same time: the lower track is faster.

Important addition

The argument relies on the hill and the dip having the same shape: the graphs deviate from the constant speed $s$ by the same area $A$, and both balls have the same distance to cover.

4.5. A messenger and a moving column

Reconstructed statement

The notebook has only a sketch with $v$, $L$ and $u$. The statement and solution below are added: a column of length $L$ moves with speed $u$. A messenger rides from its rear to its front and straight back with speed $v \gt u$ (relative to the ground). How long does the trip take, and how far does the messenger travel?

§kin_messenger§

Work in the reference frame of the column. On the way forward the messenger approaches the front with speed $v-u$, on the way back approaches the rear with speed $v+u$:

$$t_1 = \frac{L}{v-u}, \qquad t_2 = \frac{L}{v+u}.$$
Answer$$t = t_1 + t_2 = \frac{2Lv}{v^2-u^2}, \qquad S = vt = \frac{2Lv^2}{v^2-u^2}.$$

If $v$ in the sketch means the speed relative to the column, one trip from end to end simply takes $L/v$.
