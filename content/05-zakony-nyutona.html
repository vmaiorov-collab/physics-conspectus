1. Dynamics and relativity of motion

Dynamics studies the causes of motion. Motion is relative: in a closed carriage moving with $\vec v = \text{const}$ one cannot measure one's own speed or even tell that one is moving.

Reference frame

1) a reference body; 2) a coordinate system; 3) a clock.

§newt_frame§

A clock is part of the reference frame because time is also measured in it: according to Einstein's relativity it need not run the same way in different frames.

2. First law

There exist inertial reference frames: frames in which a body with zero resultant force moves with constant velocity:

$$\sum\vec F = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; \vec v = \text{const}.$$

3. Second law

For a point mass in an inertial reference frame:

$$\sum\vec F = m\vec a, \qquad [F] = \text{N} = \text{kg}\cdot\text{m}/\text{s}^2.$$

1 newton is the force that gives a 1 kg body an acceleration of 1 m/s².

4. Third law

Every action has a reaction; forces come in pairs:

$$\vec F_{12} = -\vec F_{21},$$

where $\vec F_{12}$ is the force acting on body 1 from body 2. Properties of the pair:

  1. equal in magnitude;
  2. opposite in direction;
  3. directed along the line joining the points;
  4. of the same nature.

They act on different bodies, so they do not cancel each other.

6. Gravity and the normal force

$$\vec F_\text{grav} = m\vec g, \qquad g_\text{Earth} \approx 9.81\ \frac{\text{N}}{\text{kg}} = 9.81\ \frac{\text{m}}{\text{s}^2}, \qquad g_\text{Moon}\approx1.6\ \frac{\text{m}}{\text{s}^2}.$$

$g$ is the gravitational field strength and at the same time the acceleration of free fall; it depends on the planet and the distance to it.

§newt_planet§

For a body at rest on a horizontal support $N = mg$.

Terminology note

The notebook denotes $mg$ by $W$ and calls it weight. Strictly, $mg$ is the force of gravity (applied to the body), while weight is the force with which the body presses on a support or pulls on a hanger. At rest their magnitudes are equal; in an accelerating lift they are not. The pair “$N$ and $mg$” is not a third-law pair.

7. String tension

Tension $T$ is the force with which a string acts on a body. If the string is taut, the tension is directed along it; if it is slack, $T = 0$. More in the “Tension and pulleys” notes.
