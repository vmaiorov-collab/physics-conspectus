1. Acceleration

$$\vec a = \frac{\Delta\vec v}{\Delta t}, \qquad \Delta t \to 0.$$

Acceleration is non-zero in two cases: the magnitude of the velocity changes or its direction changes.

2. Deriving x(t) at constant acceleration

Motion along a line: at $t=0$ the body is at $x_0$, has velocity $v_0$ and acceleration $a$.

§acc_axis§

For $a_x = \text{const}$ we have $a_x = \dfrac{v_x - v_{0x}}{t}$, hence

$$v_x(t) = v_{0x} + a_x t.$$

Displacement is the area under the velocity graph, that is, the area of a trapezium:

§acc_trap§ $$\Delta x = \int_0^t v_x\,dt = \frac{v_{0x} + v_x(t)}{2}\,t, \qquad \langle v_x\rangle = \frac{v_{0x}+v_x(t)}{2}.$$

Substituting $v_x(t)$:

Important$$x(t) = x_0 + v_{0x}t + \frac{a_x t^2}{2}$$

The graph of $x(t)$ is a parabola. For $v_{0x}\gt0,\ a_x\gt0$ the position grows from the start; for $v_{0x}\lt0,\ a_x\gt0$ the body first moves back, reaches a minimum and turns around.

§acc_cases§

3. The time-free formula

Express $t$ from $v_x = v_{0x} + a_x t$ and substitute it into the area formula:

$$\Delta x = \frac{v_x^2 - v_{0x}^2}{2a_x}.$$

In detail: $\Delta x = \dfrac{v_{0x}+v_x}{2}\,t$ and $t = \dfrac{v_x - v_{0x}}{a_x}$, and the product $(v_x+v_{0x})(v_x-v_{0x})$ is a difference of squares. If the final velocity is known instead of the initial one, $\Delta x = v_x t - \dfrac{a_x t^2}{2}$.

So the velocity squared depends linearly on displacement:

§acc_v2§

Speed at the middle of the path

A body accelerates from $v_0 = 1$ m/s to $v = 7$ m/s. What is the speed $v_1$ at the middle of the segment?

Whole path: $\Delta x = \dfrac{49 - 1}{2a} = \dfrac{24}{a}$. Half: $\dfrac{\Delta x}{2} = \dfrac{v_1^2 - 1}{2a}$. Dividing the first by the second:

$$2 = \frac{48}{v_1^2 - 1} \;\Rightarrow\; v_1 = 5\ \text{m/s}.$$

Braking: speed at half of the braking distance

$S = \dfrac{0 - v_0^2}{-2a}$ and $\dfrac S2 = \dfrac{v_1^2 - v_0^2}{-2a}$. Their ratio gives $2v_1^2 = v_0^2$, that is, $v_0 = v_1\sqrt2$.

Braking distance of 100 m

The braking distance is $S = 100$ m, the speed at its middle is $10$ m/s, at the end it is zero. Find the initial speed, the acceleration and the braking time.

§acc_brake§

From the previous problem $v_0 = 10\sqrt2 \approx 14.1$ m/s. Then

$$a = \frac{v_0^2}{2S} = \frac{200}{200} = 1\ \text{m/s}^2, \qquad t = \frac{2S}{v_0} = \frac{200}{10\sqrt2} \approx 14.1\ \text{s}.$$

4. Braking and turning back

The initial velocity $v_0$ is along the axis, the acceleration $a$ against it. Find $d_x(t)$ and the distance $S(t)$.

$$d_x(t) = v_0 t - \frac{at^2}{2}.$$

The body stops and turns around at the vertex of the parabola: $t_0 = v_0/a$. Until then distance equals displacement; afterwards it keeps growing:

$$S(t) = \begin{cases} v_0 t - \dfrac{at^2}{2}, & t \le t_0,\\[2mm] \Big(v_0 t_0 - \dfrac{at_0^2}{2}\Big) + \dfrac{a(t-t_0)^2}{2}, & t \gt t_0. \end{cases}$$ §acc_back§

5. Distances in equal time intervals

Start from rest, intervals of length $\tau$. The areas under the line $v = at$ for the first, second and third intervals are $\frac{a\tau^2}{2}$, $3\cdot\frac{a\tau^2}{2}$, $5\cdot\frac{a\tau^2}{2}$:

$$S_1 : S_2 : S_3 : \ldots = 1 : 3 : 5 : \ldots$$ §acc_odd§

The same holds for a ball on an inclined chute: the segments covered in equal times are in the ratio of consecutive odd numbers.

6. Two-stage acceleration

First acceleration $a_1$ during $t_1$, then $a_2$ during $t_2$. After the first stage the speed is $v_1 = a_1 t_1$, which is the initial speed of the second:

§acc_two§ $$S = \frac{a_1t_1^2}{2} + a_1t_1t_2 + \frac{a_2t_2^2}{2}.$$
